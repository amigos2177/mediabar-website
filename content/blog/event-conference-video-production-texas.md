Event video planning starts with the audience, agenda, and finished content you need. Agree on recorded coverage, interviews, live delivery, editing, and approval responsibilities before choosing a camera count or comparing estimates.

For a production partner, explore our [event and conference video production services](/video-production/events). ## Define the deliverables and priority moments

Choose which parts of the event need a complete recording and which need selected highlights. A recap, a full keynote, and an interview have different filming and editing requirements.

Possible deliverables include:

- A recap with an agreed audience, message, and length.
- Selected keynote or panel recordings, with camera, audio, and presentation requirements.
- Scheduled speaker or attendee interviews, with questions and permissions.
- Social clips with agreed formats, captions, and destinations.
- Sponsor edits based on agreed moments and approvals.
- Live delivery for remote viewers, if included in the scope.

List the original editorial pieces separately from alternate aspect ratios, captions, languages, and other versions. A deliverable list should explain the work, not promise an automatic number of assets.

## Decide whether live delivery is necessary

Remote participation may call for a live program, an on-demand recording, or both. The audience and event goals should guide that decision; streaming alone does not establish an audience increase or a financial return.

If you need [live streaming production](/video-production/live-streaming), confirm the platform, connection, audio and presentation feeds, camera positions, staff, testing, backup approach, and recordings. A recorded event and a live program may share equipment, but simultaneous delivery needs its own agreed technical plan.

## Build a scope-based estimate

Review [how Media Bar scopes video production pricing](/pricing), then provide the agenda and required deliverables. A useful estimate identifies:

- **Filming coverage:** event days, setup and rehearsal access, concurrent rooms, interviews, and priority activities.
- **Crew and equipment:** responsibilities, camera positions, audio recording, lighting, presentation capture, and backups.
- **Venue services:** AV coordination, internet, power, access requirements, and any venue charges.
- **Post-production:** story edits, session recordings, graphics, captions, versions, review rounds, and delivery requirements.
- **Travel and scheduling:** location, parking, load-in, travel time, accommodation when needed, and crew availability.

A Texas-based crew can still incur travel, parking, venue, or accommodation costs. Confirm inclusions and exclusions in the estimate rather than assuming these costs disappear with a local provider. No standard event package price is set by this guide.

## Coordinate the venue and run of show

Contact the crew while the agenda and venue arrangements are still flexible. Availability and planning time depend on dates, coverage, staffing, and venue requirements; there is no universal booking window.

Share room plans, the run of show, speaker schedules, interview availability, and organizer/venue contacts. Flag simultaneous sessions so the team can plan coverage without treating one operator as coverage for multiple rooms.

Arrange a walkthrough or a documented technical review as appropriate. Confirm setup access, camera locations, cable paths, power, audio and slide feeds, and venue service requirements. For local detail, use the [San Antonio conference venue checklist](/blog/san-antonio-conference-video-services).

## Agree on delivery and review milestones

Delivery timing is quoted by project scope. Confirm priority edits, complete recordings, captions, versions, reviewers, revision rounds, and final deadlines before filming.

Same-day or next-day content requires an agreed staffing and editing plan, available brand assets, a footage handoff process, and an available approver. It is not automatically included. If the agenda, deliverables, or review dates change, confirm the effect on cost and timing before proceeding.

## Questions to ask before hiring a crew

Ask for examples relevant to your event, a clear coverage plan for concurrent sessions, the audio and backup approach, the review workflow, and a written breakdown of deliverables. Clarify which work belongs to the video team, venue AV provider, organizer, or streaming platform.

### How many cameras do we need?

Start with the rooms, sessions, and shots you need. The plan may require multiple angles, roaming coverage, or separate interview staffing. Camera count alone does not establish complete coverage.

### Can recorded coverage and live delivery happen together?

Potentially, when both are scoped and the venue, platform, crew, feeds, and connection support the plan. Confirm testing and recording requirements before committing to simultaneous delivery.

### What should we send for an estimate?

Share the event location and dates, agenda, room layout, audience, priority sessions, interviews, intended deliverables, live requirements, budget expectations, and deadlines. Identify the venue and review contacts.
