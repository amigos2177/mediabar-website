## Start With the Audience and Communication Goal

A patient story, provider introduction, staff-training film, and recruiting video serve different audiences. Define what the viewer should understand before choosing interview participants, locations, runtime, or distribution channels.

For provider-led answers, choose topics with your healthcare team and agree on who will review the wording. Our [provider Q&A video guide](/blog/healthcare-provider-video) offers a focused planning starting point.

## A Portfolio Example: Wound Local Patient Story

Media Bar Productions handled the full production of the [Wound Local Patient Story](/work/watch/wound-local-patient-story), the featured patient-story example on our [Medical page](/video-production/medical).

For a patient-story brief of your own, define the story’s purpose, the participant’s available time, the approved filming environment, and the review process. Use the finished film as a format reference while shaping a scope around your organization’s needs.

## Choose the Format Before the Shot List

- **Provider introduction:** introduce the person, their role, and the information viewers need before contacting the organization.
- **Patient story:** plan an interview and supporting visuals around the participant’s experience and the organization’s approved brief.
- **Educational answer:** focus on one topic, with language and any graphics reviewed by the appropriate healthcare stakeholders.
- **Recruiting or internal communication:** explain the people, facilities, or work relevant to employees and prospective hires.
- **Training or product content:** define the intended users, subject specialist, demonstration requirements, and review process before quoting production.

These are planning options. The proposal should specify the format, responsibilities, and deliverables selected for your project.

## Confirm Access, Participants, and Review Responsibilities

The healthcare organization defines its privacy, consent, legal, clinical, and regulatory requirements. Before filming, ask the designated client stakeholders to confirm:

- Which spaces and activities are approved for filming.
- Who may participate and how permissions will be managed.
- What may appear or be heard in the recorded material.
- How the crew should work around facility operations and participant needs.
- Who will review the script, footage, terminology, graphics, and final edits.
- The agreed handling, transfer, retention, and delivery arrangements for project files.

Record those decisions in the production brief and communicate changes before filming. For more preparation questions, use the [controlled-location production FAQ](/resources/video-production-faq#how-to-plan-video-in-medical-industrial-or-controlled-locations).

## Prepare Interviews and Supporting Footage

Build interview questions around the communication goal and the participant’s role. Agree on the topics and time available rather than expecting participants to deliver a memorized script. Our [interview production service](/video-production/interview) explains the production approach.

Plan supporting footage with the client: the people, spaces, and activities needed to explain the story, along with any restrictions on what can be captured. Facility access and operational schedules should shape the filming plan.

## Scope the Deliverables, Cost, and Timing Together

Specify the main film, any shorter edits, captions, graphics, aspect ratios, and delivery destinations before production. Additional versions need to be included in the agreed scope.

Pricing and delivery timing depend on preparation, participants, access, filming days, locations, editing, review stages, and final versions. Agree on the review team and feedback schedule when setting the project timeline.

A useful first brief includes:

1. The audience and communication goal.
2. The intended format and where it will be used.
3. Participants, filming locations, and access requirements.
4. Client stakeholders responsible for requirements and approvals.
5. Expected deliverables, target launch date, and budget considerations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How should privacy be planned for a healthcare video?**
The healthcare organization should define its privacy, consent, legal, clinical, and regulatory requirements. The production team can then plan approved spaces, participants, visuals, and review stages around those requirements.

**Can we film in active patient-care areas?**
Filming depends on facility approval, access restrictions, participant permissions, operational needs, and the organization’s requirements. Confirm the permitted locations and schedule before production.

**Will our doctors need to be good on camera?**
An interview-style setup can help providers explain their work in their own words. Plan the questions, available time, and review responsibilities with the participants before filming.

**What healthcare videos should we make first?**
Start with the audience and communication goal. A provider introduction, patient story, educational answer, recruiting film, or internal video may suit different needs. Choose the format and placement during planning.

**Who should review the content?**
Ask the healthcare organization to identify the communications, clinical, privacy, legal, or other stakeholders needed for its project. Agree on review stages and final approval responsibility in the production brief.

**How long do healthcare videos need to be?**
Runtime depends on the audience, subject, and placement. Define the main film and any shorter versions before filming so interviews, visuals, captions, and editing support those uses.

**How much does medical video production cost?**
Every healthcare video is quoted around its audience, format, access requirements, participants, filming schedule, review process, and deliverables. Delivery timing is also quoted by scope.
