# Media Bar Keyword-to-URL Ownership Map

Last reviewed: 2026-09-04

This document assigns one primary Media Bar URL to each important organic-search intent. It is the guardrail for page creation, metadata, internal linking, blog topics, Media Bar Answers, and future SEO work.

The goal is not to force one page to be the only page that mentions a term. The goal is to make one URL the clearest primary answer for each intent while other relevant pages support it.

## Ownership rules

1. **One primary owner per search intent.** Supporting pages may mention the topic, but should link to the primary owner when useful.
2. **Money pages own hire/quote/service queries.** Blog posts and answer pages own how/why/planning questions.
3. **Do not create a new landing page for a synonym.** A new page needs a genuinely different buyer problem, audience, geography, or service.
4. **Homepage and location pages have different jobs.** The homepage owns broad San Antonio video-production demand; the San Antonio location page owns production-company/local-partner demand.
5. **Service pages own service-specific transactional queries.** Supporting guides should not use metadata that duplicates the service page's core commercial term.
6. **Pricing owns general cost/estimate intent.** Narrow answer pages may own a specific cost question such as corporate-video cost or TV-commercial cost.
7. **Careers is for collaborators.** It should not target clients looking to hire a crew.
8. **FilmCrewSanAntonio.com owns crew-only demand.** Camera crew, film crew, video crew, and agency production-support intent should be routed there instead of creating competing Media Bar pages.
9. **Location-service combinations roll up to the metro page unless evidence justifies a dedicated route.** Do not create dozens of `/austin/corporate-video` variants without GSC/SERP evidence.
10. **Case studies and watch pages are proof, not duplicate service pages.** They should reinforce their related service with descriptive internal links.

## Internal-link anchor rules

Use descriptive anchors that reinforce the assigned owner instead of repeating one phrase everywhere.

- To `/`: “San Antonio video production” / “Media Bar Productions”
- To `/locations/san-antonio`: “San Antonio video production company” / “local San Antonio production partner”
- To `/video-production`: “full-service video production” / “video production services”
- To `/video-production/corporate`: “corporate video production” / “corporate films”
- To `/video-production/commercials`: “commercial video production” / “TV and digital commercials”
- To `/video-production/events`: “event and conference video production”
- To `/video-production/live-streaming`: “live streaming and webcast production”
- To `/video-production/interview`: “interview and testimonial video production”
- To `/video-production/medical`: “medical and healthcare video production”
- To `/video-production/post-production`: “video post-production and editing”
- To `/pricing`: “video production pricing” / “what drives video cost”
- To `/studio`: “San Antonio production studio”

Avoid site-wide exact-match anchor stuffing. Use the ownership language where it is genuinely descriptive.

## Page-creation gate

Before creating any new SEO page, answer all five:

1. Is the search intent materially different from an existing owner?
2. Is there evidence of demand in GSC, live SERPs, lead conversations, or repeated client questions?
3. Can Media Bar provide real proof, expertise, and examples for the page?
4. Will the page strengthen rather than cannibalize an existing money page?
5. Can we explain in one sentence why a buyer needs this page instead of the current owner?

If any answer is no, strengthen the existing owner instead.
